2 Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to handle core everyday and office interactions in Dutch:

  • Greet and say goodbye in both informal everyday settings (hoi, dag, doei) and polite or time-of-day office contexts (goedemorgen, tot ziens, fijne dag!).
  • Introduce yourself and state your job role cleanly without an article (e.g., Ik ben software engineer), and ask how someone is doing (Hoe gaat het?).
  • Conjugate the verb zijn (to be) for all subjects (ik, jij/je, u, hij/zij/het, wij/we, jullie, zij/ze).
  • Apply the V2 Word Order rule in statements and inversion in yes/no questions — including dropping the -t in ben jij?
  • Make negative statements with niet and ask for basic help (Kun je mij helpen?).

4 Grammar Theory & Word Order Formulas

4.1 Greetings & Formal vs. Informal Register

Dutch uses two pronouns for "you" (singular): jij / je (informal) and u (formal). In Dutch offices, colleagues — including managers — almost always use jij / je from day one. Reserve u for older strangers, formal customer service, or official appointments.

  • Anytime informal: Hoi! / Hallo! (Hi! / Hello!) · Farewells: Doei! (informal bye), Dag! (hello or bye), Tot ziens! (goodbye, neutral/polite).
  • Time-of-day greetings: Goedemorgen (until 12:00), Goedemiddag (12:00–18:00), Goedenavond (after 18:00).

4.2 The Verb zijn (to be) & Stating Your Profession

The irregular verb zijn (to be) is the backbone of introductions. Note that Dutch has both stressed (jij, wij, zij) and unstressed everyday spoken forms (je, we, ze).

Rule — Professions without an article: When stating your job or role after zijn, Dutch omits the indefinite article een (a/an). Say Ik ben developer or Hij is manager — do not say Ik ben een developer. (You only use een if you add an adjective, e.g., een nieuwe collega.)

4.3 Word Order Formula 1: Statement (The V2 Rule)

Dutch main statements follow the strict V2 (Verb-Second) rule: the conjugated verb always sits in the second grammatical slot of the sentence.

Formula A — Standard Statement (V2 Rule)
1. SubjectIk
+
2. Verb (Pos 2)ben
+
3. Rest of sentencevandaag op kantoor.

If you move a time or place word to Position 1 for emphasis, the verb stays in Position 2 and the subject moves to Position 3 (inversion): Vandaag (1) + ben (2) + ik (3) + op kantoor.

4.4 Word Order Formula 2: Yes/No Question (Inversion)

To turn a statement into a yes/no question, place the conjugated verb in Position 1 and the subject in Position 2.

Formula B — Yes/No Question (Inversion)
1. Verb (Pos 1)Ben
+
2. Subject (Pos 2)jij
+
3. Restde nieuwe collega?
Critical jij/je Inversion Rule: In a normal statement, you say Jij bent / Je bent. When the verb comes before jij or je in a question, the final -t disappears: Ben jij...? / Ben je...? (Never Bent jij?).

4.6 Basic Negation with niet

To negate a statement with zijn, place niet (not) right after the verb and pronoun:

Formula C — Negation with niet
1. SubjectHij
+
2. Verb (Pos 2)is
+
3. Negationniet
+
4. Restde manager.

Examples: Ik ben niet thuis. (I am not at home.) · De meeting is niet vandaag. (The meeting is not today.)

4.7 Common Pitfalls

Pitfall 1 — Missing preposition with werk:
Incorrect: Ben jij werk?
Correct: Ben je op werk? / Ben je aan het werk? / Ben je op kantoor?

Pitfall 2 — Keeping the -t in jij questions:
Incorrect: Bent jij de collega?
Correct: Ben jij de collega? (The -t drops when jij/je follows the verb.)

Pitfall 3 — Adding een before a job title:
Incorrect: Ik ben een software engineer.
Correct: Ik ben software engineer.

Mijn eerste werkdag

Goedemorgen! Mijn naam is Alex. Ik ben vandaag nieuw op kantoor. Ik ben ontwikkelaar en dit is mijn eerste werkdag. Bij de koffieautomaat is een collega. "Hallo! Ik heet Sara," zegt zij. "Ben jij de nieuwe collega?" "Ja, aangenaam! Hoe gaat het?" "Goed, dank je! Welkom in het team."

14 Dutch Life & Work Mini-Guide

In the Dutch Office: Flat Hierarchy & jij/je

In the Netherlands, colleagues almost always address one another as jij / je from the very first day — regardless of seniority or job title. Calling a manager u or meneer/mevrouw in a typical office can feel overly distant. Use first names freely.

The Koffieautomaat (Coffee Machine) Ritual

Informal office bonding happens at the coffee machine. A standard morning opener is: Goedemorgen! Hoe gaat het? or on Mondays: Goedemorgen! Hoe is je weekend geweest? Keep it friendly and direct.

Saying Hello to Neighbors (de buren)

In apartment hallways, elevators, or on quiet neighborhood streets, saying Goedemorgen or Dag! to your buurman or buurvrouw is expected basic politeness.

15B. Writing Task: First-Day Slack / Teams Introduction

Imagine you just joined a Dutch team. Write a short 3–4 sentence introduction message in Dutch for the team's #general channel. Include: (1) a greeting, (2) your name, (3) your role without een, (4) where you are working today (op kantoor or thuis), and (5) a friendly closing.

Tip: Use words from the Vocabulary table (e.g., Goedemorgen, collega's, Ik heet, Ik ben, op kantoor, Succes, Fijne dag).

Model Answer (Slack Introduction):

Goedemorgen collega's! Mijn naam is Alex. Ik ben de nieuwe software engineer in het team. Ik ben vandaag op kantoor. Fijne dag en succes vandaag!
